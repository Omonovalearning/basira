The Illusion of Time: Why Years Feel Faster as We Grow Older

7 min read
By Omer

Why Does Your Childhood Feel Longer Than a Decade of Adult Life?

We all feel it: childhood summers seemed endless, yet the last several years passed in a flash. This isn't just emotional nostalgia — it's a documented psychological and neurological phenomenon, and scientists are finally beginning to understand why.

Theory One: Novelty and Memory Storage

When you're a child, every day brings new experiences — a new school, a new friend, a new skill. Your brain works hard to store all this unfamiliar information, and that effort stretches your perception of time. As you get older, most of your day becomes familiar routine. The brain shifts into autopilot, records fewer details, and time seems shorter when you look back.

The Physicist's Theory: The Brain Slows Down Its Image Processing

Adrian Bejan, a professor of engineering at Duke University, proposed a different explanation: as we age, neural networks grow more complex, slowing signal transmission. The brain processes fewer mental "images" per second — like a film recorded at fewer frames per second, it appears to "play back faster" when you recall it later.

The Mathematical Theory: Time as a Proportion of Life

There's a simple and compelling mathematical explanation: one year to a five-year-old represents 20% of their entire life — so it feels heavy with significance. The same year to a fifty-year-old is just 2% of their story. Time hasn't changed; only its proportion of your total experience has shrunk.

What Does Recent Research Tell Us?

A 2025 study published in Communications Biology by Cambridge researchers scanned the brains of 577 people while they watched a video clip. They found that older brains shift between "neural states" less frequently — meaning the brain makes fewer distinct mental "cuts" in the movie of life, making time feel like it slipped by unnoticed.

Can You Slow Time Down?

Researchers say yes. The key is novelty. Traveling to new places, learning a different skill, changing your daily route — all of these force your brain to pay attention again and record more detail, making each day feel "heavier" and longer in memory. Mindful awareness of experience is the closest thing we have to genuinely slowing down time.
