The Goatherd Who Woke the World: The Story of Coffee's Discovery

7 min read
By Omer

Before the Cup in Your Hand

Before coffee became billions of daily cups, a hundred-billion-dollar industry, and a morning ritual for half the planet — it was just a cluster of red berries that no one paid attention to. Until the goats did.

The Legend of Kaldi: The Goats That Wouldn't Sleep

The most famous legend tells of an Ethiopian goatherd named Kaldi, who lived around the 9th century CE. One day, he noticed his flock was unusually energetic after eating red berries from a wild shrub. The goats did not sleep that night. Kaldi tried the berries himself and felt an alertness he had never known. He brought his discovery to the monks of a nearby monastery.

The Monks, the Fire, and the Aroma

According to the legend, one monk threw the berries into a fire, denouncing them. An irresistible aroma immediately filled the room. The monks quickly raked out the roasting beans, ground them, and dissolved them in hot water — and that, the story says, was history's first cup of coffee. It helped the monks stay awake through long hours of nightly prayer.

From Legend to Actual History

Kaldi's story was first written down in 1671 by a Roman scholar named Faustus Nairon — more than eight hundred years after the supposed event. The more historically reliable account suggests that coffee's first documented use as a beverage occurred in 15th-century Yemen, where Sufi monks used it to stay awake during extended meditation sessions.

The Spread: From Yemen to the World

From Yemen, coffee spread to Mecca, Cairo, and Constantinople. By the 16th century, coffeehouses had emerged as centers of intellectual and political discussion — some were even shut down by rulers who feared their influence on public order. European traders carried coffee across the Mediterranean in the 17th century, where it became a popular alternative to beer and wine as a drink of focus and wakefulness.

What Came Next?

Today, coffee is the world's second most traded commodity after oil. More than two billion cups are consumed every day. Ethiopia, the legendary home of Kaldi, remains one of the world's most important coffee producers, and the word "coffee" itself is believed to derive from the Ethiopian region of "Kaffa." A story about restless goats changed the course of human history.
